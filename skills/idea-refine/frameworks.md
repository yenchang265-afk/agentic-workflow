# Ideation Frameworks

Lenses for Phase 1. Pick the one that fits the stuck point — running the
catalogue produces variations that differ in label only.

| Reach for it when | Framework | The part that is easy to get wrong |
|---|---|---|
| An existing product needs reimagining | **SCAMPER** — substitute, combine, adapt, magnify/minimize, put to other uses, eliminate, reverse | Applied to a greenfield idea it produces nothing; it transforms something that already exists |
| Someone is anchored on a solution | **How Might We** — "how might we [outcome] for [user] without [constraint]" | The framing must be narrow enough to act on, broad enough to admit several solutions, and free of an embedded solution. "Make users happy" and "add a settings button" both fail |
| Every idea is an increment on the status quo | **First principles** — separate what is true from what is merely conventional, then rebuild from the truths | The work is listing the assumptions that feel too obvious to state; those are the ones holding the increment in place |
| It is unclear whether the right problem is being solved | **Jobs to be done** — "when I [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]", across the functional, emotional, and social job | The competing product is often outside the category. Netflix competes with sleep |
| The idea is sprawling | **Forced constraints** — one day to build it, one feature, no obvious technology, free forever, one user or a billion | The constraint has to be one you would not accept, or it changes nothing |
| A direction feels good but untested | **Pre-mortem** — it shipped twelve months ago and flopped; say why | Sort the failure modes into preventable, acceptable, and fatal. A list without that sort is just pessimism |
| The obvious approaches all look alike | **Analogous inspiration** — who has already solved a structurally similar problem | Structural, not surface. "Uber for X" is surface; "a two-sided marketplace solving trust between strangers" is structural |
